Daybook design / Food entry

Food entry

One screen, start to finish: date and meal at the top, one text box, lines under it, and a bar above the keyboard with this meal's totals, what is left today, and Save. Food names, portions and numbers come from docs/parser-grammar.md (example 1 is the brief's meal) and the bundled USDA data. The one exception is the scanned product in frame 10, which would come from Open Food Facts and is a placeholder here.

Line markers, each a word plus a shape, never color alone: none for a match (thin border); Guess (dashed border); Assumed (amount in italics with a dotted underline); one line can carry Guess and Assumed together (frame 6a); Not found (thick border, hatched, not counted); a saved meal (a double-border header with no marker word, items indented under it); Your pick; Scanned (with the Open Food Facts credit).

Two kinds of line. Typed lines come from the text box, which is their source of truth. Added lines come from anything that is not typing: a barcode scan, a recent or frequent food chip, a saved meal chip tapped while there is text or a line, and the entries of a logged meal being edited. An added line holds its food and amount itself and lives outside the text box; the app never writes food names into the box. Added lines are listed first, in the order added, then typed lines in text order. Both kinds open the same sheet when tapped.

Frame width: 360 dp (phone) 344 dp (Z Fold 5 cover screen) 673 dp (Z Fold 5 unfolded)
Speed target: log a typed 4-item meal. Target: 1 tap to open, type, 1 tap to save.
  1. Tap + Food on Home. The entry screen opens with the cursor in the box and the keyboard up (frame 1). No tap is needed to start typing.
  2. Type the meal (keystrokes, including commas, are the "type" part of the target).
  3. Tap Save in the bar above the keyboard (frame 3). The keyboard does not need closing first.
Taps: 2 (1 to open, 1 to save). Pass. Condition: every item is a match, a guess or an assumed amount. A not found item adds taps (frames 6a to 7).
Speed target: log a saved meal or "same as yesterday". Target: 2 taps from Home.
  1. Tap + Food.
  2. Tap the chip (frame 1). The meal is logged at once and the screen closes, with Undo on Home (frame 12).
Taps: 2. Pass. Conditions: the box is empty and there are no lines yet; the meal from the clock is the right one (otherwise +1 tap); the chip is in view. The rejected 3-tap design is in speed-targets.md.
Decision for Bart: what Save does when a line is not found. Chosen: nothing is saved. While the cursor is still in the unmatched item, the line is drawn as plain typing and the button reads Save (frame 6a); tapping it marks the item not found and opens its sheet. Once the cursor leaves, the line shows "Not found" and the button reads "Fix 1 item" (frame 6b), which opens the same sheet (frame 7). Nothing typed is ever dropped. Rejected: save the matched lines and keep the unmatched text as a 0 kcal "needs a food" entry on the day; faster, but the day's totals are wrong until it is fixed, and it needs a new kind of food reference.

Opened from + Food at 7:40 am. Meal comes from the clock (Breakfast until 10:30). Date is today.

  • Meal: one tap on another chip. Slots the user adds make the row scroll sideways.
  • Date: one tap on < or > moves a day. Tapping the date opens a calendar. > stops at today.

The box has focus and the keyboard is up. Dictation is the keyboard's own mic key.

Whole-meal chips (Same as yesterday, saved meals) log at once while the box is empty and there are no lines. Once there is text or a line, their label changes to "Tap adds its items" and a tap adds the items as added lines.

Recent and frequent food chips always add one added line, with the amount last logged (Egg, 2 means 2 eggs, 100 g).

Save is disabled until there is a line.

Tapping any line opens this sheet. The keyboard goes down while it is open and comes back when it closes.

  • Which food? The current pick first, then up to 7 others in rank order. 4 rows fit here; the other 4 are below and the list scrolls. Tapping a row picks it and closes the sheet. Tapping the current pick confirms the guess.
  • Amount: a number and a unit picker. The picker lists the food's own portions, g and oz, and tsp, tbsp, cup, fl oz and ml when the food can be converted. Changing a typed line's amount rewrites the amount in the text.
  • Search all foods for anything not listed.
  • Forget this match appears only on a line that came from a learned match. Not on this one.
  • Remove from meal deletes the item's text from the box, or removes an added line.

Fixing a guess: tap the line, tap the food. 2 taps.
